What I do

Search changed. Most SEO didn't.

Ranking first stopped being the whole job the moment Google started answering questions itself. A growing share of searches now end inside an AI answer — an AI Overview, a ChatGPT reply, a Perplexity summary — where there is no blue link to win, only a citation.

My work sits across both. Classic technical SEO still earns the crawl and the ranking. Generative engine optimization decides whether an LLM understands the brand well enough to name it. I run the two as one practice, and I measure both.

How that plays out
  • Technical foundation — site architecture, internal linking, crawlability and indexation, so search engines and AI crawlers can both reach the content.
  • Entity & schema — structured data, entity disambiguation and knowledge-graph consistency, so AI systems know exactly which brand they're describing.
  • Content for retrieval — answer-first formatting, semantic chunking and definitional openers, so LLMs can lift a clean, quotable passage.
  • Citation outreach — earning mentions on the listicles, review platforms and forums that LLMs cite most.
  • Measurement — brand mentions, citations and AI share of voice reported alongside traffic and rankings.
  • Conversion — CRO audits on high-intent landing pages so the traffic actually turns into something.

Common questions

The things people ask me

What is GEO, and how is it different from SEO?

Generative engine optimization is the practice of getting a brand cited inside AI-generated answers — Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini — rather than only ranking in a list of links. SEO optimizes for a crawler that indexes pages and ranks them. GEO optimizes for a model that retrieves passages and decides which sources to name. The technical foundation overlaps heavily; the content structure, entity work and measurement do not.

How do you measure GEO performance?

Against a fixed prompt set that mirrors how real buyers ask questions. I track brand mentions, citation frequency, and share of voice versus competitors across ChatGPT, Perplexity and AI Overviews, then trend it over time. The point is to make AI visibility a reported metric rather than an assumption — the same way rankings became a reported metric twenty years ago.

You built 13 tools — do you write code?

I scope, build and iterate them with Claude, then deploy through GitHub to Render.com. I'm not going to claim to be a software engineer. What I am is someone who knows the SEO problem well enough to specify it precisely, and who ships rather than files a request and waits. The tools are in daily use by an agency delivery team, which is the test that matters.
